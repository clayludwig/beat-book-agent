# ICE Enforcement & Immigration Beat Book
### Chicago Sun-Times / WBEZ Coverage Area
*A reporting guide for covering federal immigration enforcement in the Chicago area*

---

## 🗺️ Beat Overview

This beat covers the intersection of **federal immigration enforcement and Chicago-area communities** — one of the most consequential and fast-moving local stories of the current era. The Trump administration's aggressive deportation campaigns, centered locally on **Operation Midway Blitz**, have reshaped daily life for hundreds of thousands of immigrants and their families across Chicago and its suburbs.

The beat demands you understand **three overlapping worlds simultaneously:**
1. **Federal enforcement machinery** — ICE, Border Patrol, CBP, DHS, and the legal tools they use
2. **Local government response** — the mayor, Cook County State's Attorney, Cook County Board President, and their often-conflicting approaches to "sanctuary" politics
3. **Affected communities** — Latino, South Asian, Arab, and other immigrant populations whose experiences are the human core of the story

Stories on this beat range from breaking news (arrests, shootings, protests) to investigative work (detention conditions, legal challenges, use of force) to deeply human features (families separated, teens advocating for detained parents, deported business owners).

This is a beat where **trust is everything**. Many of your most important sources will only talk to you if they believe you will handle their stories with care and not put them at risk.

## 📚 Key Topics & Themes

### 1. Operation Midway Blitz — The Defining Local Story
**Operation Midway Blitz** is the name given to the Trump administration's surge in immigration enforcement in the Chicago area that began in the fall of 2025. It is the organizing event of this entire beat and the backdrop for almost every story.

- Federal agents from ICE, Border Patrol, and DHS flooded Chicago neighborhoods, suburbs, and transit hubs.
- At least **32 defendants** faced federal prosecution in Chicago tied to the operation — many charged with assaulting or resisting federal agents. **15 of the 32 have already been cleared**; grand jurors refused to indict at least three, and one was found not guilty at trial. (Jon Seidel tracks all cases in a live database — see *Sources* section.)
- The operation led to **two shootings by federal agents in Illinois**: the fatal shooting of **Silverio Villegas González** in Franklin Park in September (shot at close range during a traffic stop), and the near-fatal shooting of **Marimar Martinez** on Chicago's Southwest Side (shot five times by a Border Patrol agent).
- The operation caused widespread fear — residents in Logan Square created "safe space" Halloween blocks, federal agents deployed tear gas before a children's Halloween parade in Old Irving Park, and an agent allegedly pushed 33rd Ward Alderperson Rossana Rodriguez Sanchez while she checked on constituents.

**Reporter's note:** The Sun-Times maintains a live tracker of every known federal prosecution tied to Operation Midway Blitz. This is a key reference document to bookmark.

---

### 2. The Marimar Martinez Shooting — A Landmark Case
Marimar Martinez, a Chicago woman with a valid Illinois concealed-carry permit, was **shot five times by a Border Patrol agent** during Operation Midway Blitz. She survived. This case has become a focal point for accountability reporting.

Key facts:
- Her attorney, **Christopher Parente**, says newly released evidence shows federal agents **lied** in their account of the shooting, including drawing a diagram depicting three "blocking" vehicles that video shows did not exist.
- Agents sent text messages saying it was **"time to get aggressive"** before the shooting — messages a federal judge allowed Martinez to release publicly.
- DHS said the agent involved was "suspended" following the shooting.
- Martinez was **not charged** with any firearms offense despite being armed; she plans to file a civil lawsuit.
- She attended Trump's State of the Union address as a visible act of protest.

**Watch:** This civil lawsuit, expected to be filed, will be a major story. Court filings will be public and should be monitored at the Dirksen Federal Courthouse.

### 3. The Broadview ICE Facility — Ground Zero for Protests
The **Broadview Immigration and Customs Enforcement processing center** in suburban Broadview became the physical epicenter of local protest and advocacy.

- During Operation Midway Blitz, the facility — officially a *processing* center — operated **as a de facto detention center** without the rules and oversight required of detention facilities (a Sun-Times/WBEZ investigation finding).
- Windows were boarded up; fences and barriers were erected to keep protesters and public at bay.
- In November 2025, a **clergy-led protest outside the facility** led to 21 arrests; Cook County prosecutors later dropped charges in 19 of those cases, citing "the totality of the circumstances."
- Faith leaders were **denied access** to detainees for months. A federal judge issued a preliminary injunction in February 2026 ordering DHS to allow clergy in — and on Ash Wednesday, **Father Paul Keller** (Claretian Missionaries) and **Father Leandro Fossá** (Our Lady of Mount Carmel, Melrose Park) entered the facility for the first time in months.
- By February 2026, the facility was largely empty — a stark contrast to the crowded conditions of fall 2025.

**Address:** Broadview ICE Processing Center, 4045 S. Mannheim Rd., Broadview, IL

### 4. Local Government vs. Federal Agents — The Political Fault Lines
This beat requires understanding a three-way tension between Chicago City Hall, Cook County government, and federal authorities.

**Mayor Brandon Johnson:**
- Signed an **executive order** instructing Chicago police to investigate alleged ICE misconduct and make referrals to the state's attorney "at the direction of the Mayor's Office."
- The order was immediately called **"wholly inappropriate"** by the Cook County State's Attorney, who said it could jeopardize prosecutions by making them look politically motivated.
- Johnson later clarified he was "not trying to control" which ICE cases get referred — but the damage to the city-county relationship was done.

**Cook County State's Attorney Eileen O'Neill Burke:**
- Faced intense pressure from elected officials, immigrant advocates, and Cook County Board President Toni Preckwinkle to prosecute federal agents involved in shootings.
- Her office maintained it **lacks jurisdiction** over on-duty federal agents "except in extremely narrow circumstances."
- In February 2026, she unveiled a formal protocol for prosecuting federal agents' alleged crimes, developed in collaboration with **Illinois Attorney General Kwame Raoul** and the Illinois State's Attorneys Association.
- Key internal figure: **Yvette Loizon**, chief assistant state's attorney for policy and external affairs, who drafted the legal memos on this issue.

**Cook County Board President Toni Preckwinkle:**
- Along with more than half the county's commissioners, formally called on Burke to file charges against agents in the Silverio Villegas González and Marimar Martinez shootings.

**The core tension to watch:** Burke's office insists it cannot independently investigate federal agents and can only act on referrals from law enforcement — but Johnson's attempt to direct those referrals created a political interference problem. This unresolved jurisdictional puzzle will generate stories for months.

### 5. Federal Prosecutions and Legal Challenges — The Courts as Battlefield
Federal court is where many of the most consequential immigration battles play out. The **Dirksen Federal Building** (219 S. Dearborn St.) is the hub.

Key cases and legal threads to follow:
- **Juan Espinoza Martinez:** A Chicago man acquitted by a federal jury of offering $10,000 to kill Border Patrol Cmdr. Gregory Bovino — then immediately re-detained by immigration authorities. A federal judge in Indiana ruled his detention unlawful; he spent 30 years in Chicago, has no criminal record, and has children who are U.S. citizens.
- **The Bovino murder-for-hire plot:** Cmdr. **Gregory Bovino** led enforcement operations in both Chicago and Minneapolis. He was demoted after the killing of Alex Pretti in Minneapolis. Congress heard testimony that Bovino "escalated the situation."
- **Facebook ICE sighting page lawsuit:** Cicero small business owner **Kassandra "Kae" Rosado** sued AG Pam Bondi and DHS Secretary Kristi Noem, alleging they coerced Facebook into taking down her **"ICE Sighting-Chicagoland"** page, which had nearly 100,000 members before being shut down in October. The lawsuit, filed by the Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression (FIRE), is a First Amendment case worth tracking.
- **Special prosecutor petition:** Attorney **Sheryl Weikal** filed a petition to appoint a special prosecutor to handle cases involving alleged unlawful conduct by federal agents, arguing Burke's office has failed to act.
- **Ruben Torres Maldonado (father of Ofelia Torres Hidalgo):** Detained in Niles; released on bond after his terminally ill daughter Ofelia, a Lake View High School junior, publicly advocated for him. An immigration judge granted him conditional cancellation of removal in February 2026, offering a pathway to permanent residency. Ofelia died in February 2026.

### 6. Student and Community Protests
The protest movement has been substantial and youth-led.

- **Senn High School** (Edgewater): Students staged a schoolwide walkout in February 2026.
- Hundreds of students from **Mather, Northside College Prep, Roosevelt, Amundsen, Von Steuben, and Lake View** high schools walked out in separate demonstrations.
- A national **"spring surge to melt ICE"** protest day drew students to the Loop's Federal Plaza and neighborhood marches in Lake View, coordinated with demonstrations across the country.
- Student organizers largely used **social media** to organize — something important to monitor as a tip source.

---

### 7. Affected Communities — Beyond the Headlines
The stories of individuals and communities swept up in enforcement are the moral core of this beat.

- **South Asian/Pakistani community:** The story of **Asif Amin Cheema**, a 63-year-old Pakistani man arrested near his home in Addison and deported to Pakistan despite having applied for asylum and having no criminal record. His family — including daughter **Rabia Amin**, a law clerk — kept quiet due to community stigma. His health collapsed at the airport before deportation. His daughter's interview is a model for how to approach sources reluctant to speak. (Story by Esther Yoon-Ji Kang, WBEZ)
- **Latino community:** The broad target of enforcement, including in heavily Latino neighborhoods like Pilsen, Little Village, and the Southwest Side.
- **Muslim community:** Coverage of Chicago-area Muslims leaning on community during Ramadan amid enforcement anxiety.
- **The Cheema family's sandwich shop** nearly collapsed while he was detained — the economic ripple effects of detention on immigrant-owned small businesses is an undercovered angle.

---

### 8. National Policy Context
This local beat exists within a national framework you must understand:

- **DHS funding fight:** As of February 2026, Senate Democrats (led by Sen. Dick Durbin of Illinois) were blocking DHS funding over ICE tactics. Democrats demanded 10 reforms including: targeted enforcement with judicial warrants, a ban on agent masks, required ID display, protection of sensitive locations (schools, hospitals, churches), a ban on racial profiling, and use-of-force standards.
- **Acting ICE Director Todd Lyons** testified to Congress: "We are only getting started."
- **DHS Secretary Kristi Noem** and **AG Pam Bondi** have been active in shaping the national enforcement narrative — including allegedly pressuring Facebook to remove ICE-sighting pages.
- **Border czar Tom Homan** was sent to Minneapolis to de-escalate after the Pretti killing — his interventions (or lack thereof) in Chicago are worth tracking.
- **Illinois gun rights:** After federal agents killed Alex Pretti in Minnesota citing his legal firearm, Illinois gun law experts clarified that lawful concealed-carry holders CAN carry near protests — *unless* the protest required a government permit. Marimar Martinez had a legal gun and was never charged. This is an ongoing tension to watch.

## 🗂️ Background & Context

### What Is a "Sanctuary City"?
Chicago is an informal "sanctuary city," meaning city government policy limits cooperation with federal immigration enforcement. Specifically:
- Chicago Police Department policy (since 2012) prohibits CPD officers from asking about immigration status or detaining people solely based on civil immigration holds.
- The city does not allow use of city resources to assist federal immigration enforcement in most circumstances.
- However, the city cannot stop federal agents from operating within city limits — federal law preempts local ordinance on immigration enforcement.

### Illinois Law Relevant to This Beat
- **Illinois TRUST Act (2017):** Limits when law enforcement can honor ICE detainer requests.
- **Illinois Firearm Concealed Carry Act:** Prohibits guns at permitted public demonstrations but allows them in other encounters — key context for the Marimar Martinez and Alex Pretti cases.
- **Illinois Human Rights Act:** Prohibits discrimination based on national origin — could be relevant to racial profiling claims.

### The Broadview Facility — History
- Located at 4045 S. Mannheim Rd., Broadview, officially an ICE "processing center," not a detention facility.
- Religious immigrant advocates (including the late Sisters Pat Murphy and JoAnn Persch) used to regularly visit until COVID ended in-person visits in 2020. Access was never fully restored.
- During Operation Midway Blitz, it held detainees in crowded conditions without the procedural protections required of formal detention centers — a central finding of a Sun-Times/WBEZ investigation.

### Federal Structure
Understanding which agency did what is essential:
- **ICE (Immigration and Customs Enforcement):** Arrests, detention, and deportation of people already in the U.S.
- **CBP/Border Patrol:** Primarily a border agency, but deployed inland during Operation Midway Blitz — their presence in Chicago is legally and politically contested.
- **HSI (Homeland Security Investigations):** The investigative arm of DHS; made arrests in cases like the Espinoza Martinez/Bovino plot.
- **287(g) program:** Allows local law enforcement to be deputized as immigration agents — *check whether any suburban departments in the Chicago area have these agreements.*

### National Context
- The Trump administration framed Operation Midway Blitz and similar operations in other cities as targeting criminals, but evidence shows many arrestees had no criminal records.
- Two Americans were killed by federal agents in Minneapolis (Alex Pretti, a VA nurse, and Renee Macklin Good) — those killings radicalized local protest movements and galvanized Democrats' opposition in Congress.
- The administration responded to critics by demoting Cmdr. Gregory Bovino, but acting ICE Director Todd Lyons told Congress: "We are only getting started."

## 🛡️ Reporting Tips

### Building Trust with Immigrant Sources
- **Never promise safety you can't guarantee.** Be clear about what publication means: their name, their story, their photo online. Let sources make fully informed decisions.
- **Offer anonymity early and often** — even if a source ultimately agrees to be named, they need to know they can be anonymous. Many stories in this coverage, including the powerful Jungman Elementary lockdown account, use only first names (e.g., "Alma").
- **Use community intermediaries.** Immigrant advocates, faith leaders, and attorneys often serve as trusted bridges. Father Paul Keller (Claretian Missionaries) and Father Leandro Fossá (Our Lady of Mount Carmel) are two established contacts. The National Immigrant Justice Center (based in Chicago) is another.
- **Understand the stigma.** The Cheema family story is instructive: many immigrants (especially South Asian families) told loved ones a relative was "sick" rather than detained — to avoid gossip. Surface these dynamics explicitly with sources.
- **Speak to people in their language.** Several stories are published in Spanish — the Sun-Times/WBEZ Spanish-language unit, *La Voz*, is a partner. For South Asian communities, Urdu, Hindi, or Gujarati speakers may be needed.

### Covering Federal Law Enforcement
- **File FOIA requests early and often** with DHS, CBP, and ICE. They are slow to respond; the clock starts when you file.
- **Monitor the Dirksen Federal Courthouse** (219 S. Dearborn) for new filings: civil suits, habeas petitions, deportation orders, criminal cases. PACER is your tool (federal courts records system).
- **DHS/ICE press contacts are slow.** Always note the time and content of your request in your story. Their non-response is itself newsworthy.
- **Video is everything.** The Marimar Martinez case turned on video contradicting federal agents' written accounts. Always ask: Is there video? Bodycam, dashcam, bystander footage, surveillance? File requests for all of it.

### Covering Protests
- **Position yourself carefully.** At Broadview-type protests, you need to be visible as press (press badge, clearly identifying yourself) to avoid being swept up in arrests, but close enough to observe.
- **Get both sides at protests.** The Senn High School walkout story got both protest organizers and the teacher who resigned after supporting ICE — the range of perspectives makes coverage more credible to general Chicago readers.
- **Document arrests.** Get names, charges, and arresting agency. Cook County prosecutors dropped charges on 19 of 21 Broadview arrests — this only became a story because reporters tracked the original arrests.

### Source Verification
- For detained individuals, check: EOIR (Executive Office for Immigration Review) court records, ICE detainee locator (ice.gov/detainee-locator), and BOP inmate locator if transferred to federal prison.
- Court records at Dirksen (federal) and the Leighton Criminal Courthouse (state) are essential.

### Safety and Ethics
- Do not publish information that could help ICE identify or locate undocumented individuals.
- Do not share specific locations where immigrants are sheltering without their explicit informed consent.
- The "ICE Sighting-Chicagoland" Facebook page, before it was shut down, showed how community alerts can create a story in themselves — but publishing real-time enforcement location data could endanger people.
